## TL;DR

- Before learning the Dolphin Dive dance, you should be able to perform at least one controlled push-up.
- Practice the movement slowly without music first. Build the strength to lower your body one section at a time.
- Once you have control, perform the move in four beats: hands and leg on 1, chin on 2, push up while lowering the hips and knee on 3, then step and rise on 4.
- The key to a smooth Dolphin Dive is combining controlled sequencing with rhythm and timing.

The Dolphin Dive dance is a floor move that combines upper-body strength, controlled weight support, and precise timing. Instead of dropping to the floor all at once, you lower your chin, chest, hips, and raised knee in sequence. That controlled sequence creates the diving motion.

This tutorial covers the [House Dance](https://dancewithceech.com/house-dance) floorwork version. In the demonstration below, hip-hop pioneer [Buddha Stretch](https://broadwaydancecenter.com/faculty/buddha-stretch) of Elite Force explains the movement while house-dance pioneer [Caleaf Sellers](https://www.peridance.com/faculty/caleaf-sellers) demonstrates the Dolphin Dive.

[Watch the demonstration](https://youtu.be/2ScBJ-zBMwM)

## What is the Dolphin Dive dance?

The Dolphin Dive dance is a controlled floor sequence in which you support your weight with your hands, lower your body in sections, push your upper body back up, and step out of the movement.

The name makes sense when you watch the body travel through the sequence. The upper and lower sections do not land at the same time. One part follows another, creating a wave-like path through the floor.

The version taught here follows Caleaf’s demonstration. Other dancers may use the same name for related movements with different setups or exits.

## Can complete beginners learn the Dolphin Dive?

Complete beginners can work toward the Dolphin Dive, but they may need to develop upper-body strength before attempting the complete move.

My minimum prerequisite is the ability to perform a push-up. If you cannot control your body during a push-up, you probably will not have enough strength yet to control the descent of the Dolphin Dive.

That does not mean you cannot begin learning. It means your first stage is strength preparation instead of attempting the complete floor move.

Dance readiness is different for each person. Professional [dance assessments](https://nyulangone.org/care-services/harkness-center-for-dance-injuries/dance-injury-prevention-programs) consider factors such as strength, flexibility, and technique. Being able to do a push-up is my practical minimum for this specific movement, but it is not a guarantee against injury.

If you are still building that strength, continue practicing your push-ups and work on a [standing house move](https://dancewithceech.com/blog/house-dance-jack-in-the-box) instead of forcing the floor sequence.

If you have pain, an injury, or a physical limitation, consult an appropriate healthcare professional before trying the move.

## How should you practice before adding music?

Practice the Dolphin Dive slowly and without worrying about rhythm. The goal at this stage is to develop technique, strength, and control.

This is not a slower performance version of the move. It is preparation for performing the full move with rhythm later.

### 1. Place both hands on the floor

Begin by bringing both hands down to the floor, similar to the preparation for a handstand.

Lift one leg into the air behind you. Your hands now support much of your upper-body weight while the raised leg prepares the lower half of the movement.

### 2. Lower your chin toward the floor

Bend your arms and slowly bring your chin close to the floor.

Your chin should not touch. Stop just above the floor.

If you cannot stop the descent, continue developing your push-up strength before trying to move faster.

### 3. Let your chest follow

After the chin lowers, allow the chest to follow.

Do not drop your chin and chest together as one heavy section. The sequence should begin to travel through your body.

Think:

**Chin, then chest.**

### 4. Lower your hips and raised knee

Continue the movement by allowing your hips to descend.

The knee of the leg that was raised in the air follows the hips toward the floor. Keep that knee just above the ground. Do not let it strike or rest on the floor.

The complete downward sequence is:

**Chin, chest, hips, then raised knee.**

### 5. Push your upper body up

As your hips and raised knee move close to the floor, begin pushing your upper body up.

Straighten your arms, lift your head, and raise your chest. The upper-body position resembles an Upward-Facing Dog shape through the head, chest, and straight arms, but it is not the complete yoga pose. Keep your hips close to the ground as the lower body finishes descending.

This creates the most important opposing action in the Dolphin Dive: your upper body rises while your hips and raised knee are still descending.

### 6. Step down with the raised foot

Bring down the foot of the leg that had been in the air and step onto it.

This transfers the pressure away from your hands and allows you to stand back up. Once you are standing, you are ready to begin another Dolphin Dive.

## How do you perform the Dolphin Dive dance in four beats?

In Caleaf’s demonstration, one Dolphin Dive fits into a four-beat bar.

### Beat 1: Hands down and leg up

Place both hands on the floor and lift one leg into the air on the same beat.

These two actions happen together. Do not place your hands first and lift the leg as a separate count.

### Beat 2: Chin down

Lower your chin close to the floor without touching it.

Your arms bend and control the descent.

### Beat 3: Push up while the hips and knee descend

Push through your hands, straighten your arms, and lift your chin and chest.

At the same time, allow your hips and the knee of the raised leg to move close to the floor. Keep the knee just above the ground.

### Beat 4: Step and stand

Bring the raised foot down and step onto it.

Use that step to remove pressure from your hands and rise back to standing. You should be ready to place your hands down again on the next beat 1.

The count is:

1. **Hands down, leg up**
2. **Chin down**
3. **Chest up, hips and knee down**
4. **Step and stand**

## How do you make a smooth Dolphin Dive?

A smooth Dolphin Dive requires two kinds of control: physical control and rhythmic control.

Physical control allows you to lower each section of your body without collapsing. Rhythmic control places that sequence inside the music.

The most useful cue is:

**Lower in sequence, then lift the chest while the hips and knee are still descending.**

That opposing action on beat 3 helps the movement flow. If the chest, hips, and knee all move down together, the move looks like a drop. If the upper body begins to rise as the lower body finishes descending, the movement looks more like a continuous dive.

Do not rush to practice with music. First, make sure you can control the complete sequence slowly:

**Chin, chest, hips, knee, chest up, step.**

Once the technique and strength are present, you must add rhythm. A controlled movement performed outside the count will still look disconnected from the music.

If counting the music is more difficult than remembering the shape, you can [practice timing](https://dancewithceech.com/beat-first) separately. BeatFirst isolates rhythm and taps. It does not teach the Dolphin Dive technique itself.

## What happens if you descend without enough control?

Descending too quickly can cause the raised knee or another part of the body to hit the floor harder than intended.

I once had a student come down too fast and too hard. His raised knee struck the ground and caused an immediate bruise.

That experience is why I emphasize slow, controlled preparation. Do not worry about matching the rhythm during this stage. First develop enough strength to stop your chin and knee just above the floor.

After you can control the descent, begin working toward the four-beat timing.

## Common Dolphin Dive corrections

| What you notice | Likely issue | What to correct |
|---|---|---|
| Your chin touches the floor | You are not controlling the descent | Practice more slowly and strengthen the lowering portion of your push-up |
| Your raised knee strikes the floor | Your lower body is dropping too quickly | Stop the raised knee just above the ground |
| The move looks like separate poses | The body sections are not connected | Practice the order: chin, chest, hips, then knee |
| The move is controlled but looks off beat | The timing is not matching the music | Return to the four-beat cycle |
| Your hands still carry your weight on beat 4 | The recovery step is incomplete | Step down with the raised foot before standing |
| Both hands go down before the leg lifts | Beat 1 has been divided into two actions | Place the hands down and lift the leg on the same beat |
| Your whole body drops at once | You are skipping the sequence | Slow down and practice one body section at a time |

## Does “dolphin diving” always mean this dance move?

No. The words “dolphin dive” and “dolphin diving” can describe different actions depending on the activity.

They may refer to movements in swimming, sports, games, or other dance styles. In this tutorial, Dolphin Dive refers specifically to the floor move demonstrated by Caleaf: hands down, one leg raised, controlled lowering, chest lifted, then a step back to standing.

This distinction matters when you search for tutorials. Adding words such as “dance,” “house dance,” or “floor move” can help you find the movement covered here.

## What should you practice next?

Build the Dolphin Dive in the following order:

1. Develop enough strength to perform a controlled push-up.
2. Practice the floor technique slowly without music.
3. Lower the chin, chest, hips, and raised knee in sequence.
4. Stop the chin and raised knee just above the floor.
5. Push the chest up while the hips and knee finish descending.
6. Step down with the raised foot and stand.
7. Add the four-beat rhythm only after you can control the complete sequence.
8. Stop when your control begins to break down instead of forcing more repetitions.

Complete beginners may spend more time developing the prerequisite strength. Dancers who already have that strength can focus on connecting the sequence and fitting it into the four-count.

If you need help identifying exactly where your movement is breaking down, [individual feedback](https://dancewithceech.com/private-lessons) can provide direct diagnosis, concrete corrections, and custom drills presented one step at a time.
