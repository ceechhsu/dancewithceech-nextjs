# Complete dance audio alignment

## Version 4 — complete dance section, October 4, 2026

The spoken count-in no longer selects or refines the on-device audio offset. The matcher compares the entire fixed musical section: all 16 dance beats at the catalog tempo, beginning after the four count-in beats and ending after the last dance beat. The dance interval is never shortened to fit a student recording. Its endpoint uses the envelope's complete-bin convention, so a fractional final millisecond does not require a nonexistent extra decoded bin.

The full-section correlation selects one constant offset. Every beat must have nonconstant recorded audio and a matching waveform near that same clock, with next-beat smoothing excluded from its local check. Fifteen overlapping two-beat sections verify timing throughout the complete dance; their offsets must remain within a 30 ms spread. They verify the offset rather than moving individual steps. Candidate matches that continue the reference's repeating musical cadence past the purported ending are rejected, and competing complete sections still require a clear correlation lead. These are signal-quality heuristics, not confidence probabilities or proof of camera-to-microphone synchronization.

Uncertain content matches stop before step analysis or scoring and show **Record another take** and **Choose another recording**, while keeping a download of the original recording available. Accepted challenges retain their challenge context on the retake link and require fresh recording. Decoder errors and interrupted analysis retain retry. Verified owner-confirmed manual synchronization continues to take priority; earlier automatic alignment versions are rechecked when a saved comparison is opened, while reusable foot tracking is retained.

Verification includes independent count-in waveforms, complete repeating music, missing first/final beats, isolated shifted beats (including a quieter beat), timing changes, stereo microphone gain/sample-rate differences, and an exact fractional endpoint regression. Replaying ten earlier real pairs retains their expected dance offsets and rejects the pair with a changing dance offset. The latest failed Marching upload establishes a -256 ms offset from its complete dance section; the count-in ambiguity is no longer an acceptance gate. Physical-phone confirmation of the installed update remains a separate check.
