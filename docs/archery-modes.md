# Archery scoring, challenges and tournaments

Updated October 27, 2026. This extends the private classroom work described in [class-progress.md](class-progress.md). Current release status is recorded in [archery-live-release.md](archery-live-release.md); the dated preview and readiness sections below describe development history.

## Phone preview

The isolated preview is deployed in the existing DanceWithCeech Vercel project as a **Preview** environment, with `ARCHERY_DEMO_ONLY=true` and `ATTENDANCE_ENABLED=false`. Its root opens the fictional archery preview; real class, account, payment and other application routes are denied by the preview guard. Production domains, settings and DNS were not changed. The staging source was exported from the committed website and overlaid only with the classroom changes, excluding unrelated uncommitted work and environment files.

The user-facing temporary share link is provided in the chat and lasts seven days. It applies only to the dedicated `dwc-archery-phone-preview.vercel.app` preview address, leaving project-wide deployment protection intact. Future reviewed preview updates can reassign this dedicated preview alias so the phone link continues to work. Do not reassign production or other project aliases.

The preview uses the same interface and PostgreSQL rules as the integrated class app, running in an isolated browser database with fictional names. Use **Preview as** to switch between students and the instructor. Changes persist in that browser using IndexedDB. Use one tab; the preview does not synchronize across devices. **Preview options → Reset preview** clears only the fictional browser data. The first visit downloads the preview engine, approximately 13 MB before compression. The real app does not download this engine or rely on a browser database.

Try this sequence:

1. Open the monthly calendar. Today is outlined; tap it to see the daily round-summary table immediately. To add a round, choose **Individual scoring**, enter distance and tap an arrow. Spin the target-colored wheel or tap its ring, then confirm the displayed score. Scores 10, 10, 9, 8, 7 total 44; that date gains a practice marker. Arrow counts and distance remain adjustable.
2. To challenge a classmate, choose a calendar date and tap **Challenge a classmate**, tap the classmate field and scroll the vertical wheel. Confirm a name, enter the game and send. The wheel lists all students; your own name is marked “you” and cannot be challenged.
3. Switch **Preview as** to Instructor, tap **Today → Host a tournament**. Enter a name, game, format (single or double elimination), and optional instructions such as “Best of three. First to two wins.” Choose a signup timer or manual closure, then open registration. You can optionally join as the instructor.
4. Switch between the sample students, choose **Today**, and tap **Enter tournament** on the tournament card. Students can leave while registration remains open. This browser-only preview simulates classmates through the role selector; phones do not share its sample database.
5. As Instructor, use **Close registration & draw**. Only opted-in players enter the random draw. Eleven entrants produce sixteen slots and five first-round byes. Timed signup closes and draws automatically on the next request/poll (within the normal five-second refresh while a page is open). The instructor can close early or use manual signup with no timer.
6. As a joined student, open the tournament to see **Your next match**, the opponent, and instructions to find each other. Report a winner, optionally adding a score. Switch to the opponent to confirm or dispute; confirmation advances the winner automatically. A dispute pauses that result and tells both players to find the instructor. Switch to Instructor and use Resolve disputes to choose the winner. The instructor can resolve other students’ results directly; when competing, the instructor also needs opponent confirmation. Everyone in the class can follow the board. On phones, round buttons keep the board compact; larger screens show columns.
7. Return to the calendar and select a day to review every saved round for that date, plus all its tournaments. Dates in the future cannot record practice; hosting is available for today. **Challenge a classmate** is a separate student button; the instructor has **Review class challenges**.

Students have no permanent Tournament button and see no empty tournament message. Tournament cards appear directly on the selected date only when events exist. Open signup offers **Enter tournament**, joined players can leave until closure, and active/completed cards show opponents, brackets and champions. Challenge notifications have a **View challenges** shortcut that also works before choosing a day. Instructor hosting is a separate **Host a tournament** button, with new tournaments brought into view after creation.

The preview upgrades its existing browser database in place, retaining previous scorecards and draws. A fresh or reset preview has no pre-created tournament. Preview controls are separate from the signed-in classroom interface and do not appear there.

## Calendar and registration

The initial calendar defaults to the current month in the class timezone and highlights today. Select a date before choosing a mode. Practice markers cover all saved round dates; tournament markers cover all class event dates. Selecting a date immediately opens a summary table above the mode buttons, with each round’s distance, arrow count, individual target marks and total. Students need not open Individual scoring or scroll below its form to review scores. Date-scoped queries return all rounds for that day rather than filtering the recent 500-round list; students still cannot see classmates’ private practice scores.

Only the class instructor can open, close, draw or cancel an event. Signup can use a 1–120 minute timer (default five minutes), or stay open until manually closed. The instructor may close early. Draws accept 2–64 players, including optional instructor entry. Each student join/leave uses their verified enrollment; instructor entry uses a separate class-scoped competitor bound to verified email and Google subject. No enrollment or attendance is created. Supplied participant or identity fields cannot enroll someone else. Closing freezes entrants, rechecks active enrollment and creates opponents once. Retried close requests preserve the same draw. Closing with zero or one entrant records a closed event without a bracket or champion. Class row locks serialize joining and closing; late joins are rejected by the server.

## Individual scoring

- Five arrows initially; adjustable from 1 to 100 per round. Distance is always selected by the student, in meters or yards; no fixed class distance is assumed.
- Score entry uses a spinning wheel with a fixed top marker: M (miss, 0), 1–10, and X (inner ten, 10). Blank arrows are not implicitly scored as X. White: 1–2; black: 3–4; blue: 5–6; red: 7–8; yellow: 9, 10 and X; M is neutral gray. The exact X/M labels and numeric point values are both retained. Legacy zeros display as M; legacy tens remain 10 because an X cannot be inferred.
- Drag with touch or mouse, tap a segment, use the previous/next buttons, or operate the focused wheel with arrow keys (Home=M, End=X). Confirm explicitly; Cancel/Escape leaves the current arrow unchanged. Opening a scored arrow restores its value, and confirmation moves focus to the next arrow. Saving requires a score for every selected arrow.
- The total and maximum are computed automatically. Date, distance, unit and optional notes are retained with the arrow marks. The native dialog traps focus and restores it on close; keyboard operation, touch cancellation, reduced motion and small-phone layouts are covered.
- Students can enter rounds within their current class/enrollment dates, up to today in the class timezone. The instructor can review their class; classmates cannot see each other's individual scores.
- The original freeform practice journal remains available under **Earlier practice notes**, preserving previous entries. Weight training keeps its existing workout journal.

## Instructor personal practice

The instructor can choose **Today → Individual scoring** and use the same five-arrow default, adjustable count/distance, meters/yards, M/X score wheel and notes as students. No student-view switch or enrollment is needed. The date view shows **Your practice** separately from **Student practice**; the scorecard also keeps personal history separate from student review. Calendar markers include both personal rounds and class-review activity for the instructor, while student calendars do not reveal instructor practice.

Instructor rounds are stored in a separate service-only table and bound to the verified class owner’s email and Google subject. The app never takes practitioner identity from the request body. Students and other instructors cannot read the rounds, notes or day counts. A new owner of the class does not gain access to the previous instructor’s personal history. Totals are calculated from arrow scores, exact M/X marks persist, and repeated saves are idempotent. Existing saved rounds cannot be overwritten. Dates follow class dates and timezone, up to today; archived history remains readable, with new writes denied while the class is archived.

The preview upgrades existing browser data atomically without clearing student rounds or brackets. Upgrade tests cover each earlier preview schema and rollback after an injected failure. Production migrations remain unapplied.

## Challenges and results

Students see only names and enrollment IDs for active classmates in the same archery class. The classmate field opens a compact vertical wheel with five visible rows and a highlighted choice. Scroll with touch or mouse, tap a row, or use arrow/Home/End/Page keys, then confirm. Cancel/Escape preserves the previous choice. Every active student is listed, including the current student marked “you”; self-challenges are disabled. The picker uses the full class roster with no name limit, and excludes removed, dropped, future and other-class enrollments. Class/semester membership comes from the selected class and its enrollment dates, rather than a sample-name list in the component. The isolated preview continues to use fictional names; the signed-in class app reads its verified semester roster. A classmate chooses whether to accept a challenge. Invitations update every five seconds while the page is open and when the window regains focus. This version does not send email or device push notifications.

Only the two players and the class instructor can access the challenge. A player can propose a winner; the other player must confirm it or raise a dispute for an instructor decision. Either participant must be the winner, and no score is required. The instructor can finalize an accepted challenge when needed. An active pair cannot accumulate duplicate invitations, and senders are limited to five challenges per five minutes. Cancellation/decline closes the invitation without awarding a win. Responses do not reveal a peer's email or provider identity.

## Bracket research and choice

World Archery uses seeded match-play charts with byes permitted; positions typically come from a qualification ranking. The ITF's draw rules award byes to seeds first, with additional byes drawn by lot. These are professional conventions, rather than a requirement that a classroom game use qualification scores.

Sources: [World Archery 2026 event rules](https://extranet.worldarchery.sport/documents/index.php/Rules/Rule_Book_versions/2026-01-27/EN-Book_2_-_2026-01-27_Version.pdf), [ITF Masters draw regulations](https://m.itftennis.com/media/13658/2025-itf-masters-world-tennis-tour-regulations-final.pdf).

For this requested **random class draw**, use the next power of two as the bracket size and subtract the number of entrants to find the byes. Randomize names into mirrored seed slots to spread those byes through the first round. Do not pair a bye against a bye or reshuffle after results.

| 7 | 8 | 1 | 6 |

| 16 | 16 | 0 | 15 |

Only the class instructor creates draws. Players report their own match results, the opponent confirms or disputes, and the instructor can resolve a result directly. The bracket is class-visible. Confirmed wins advance automatically; single elimination exits after one match loss, while double elimination exits after two. A first double-elimination loss enters the fixed second-chance path. Main and second-chance winners meet in the championship; if the second-chance winner wins, a deciding championship rematch gives both finalists two-loss protection. Byes never count as played wins or losses. Resolve a tie in the chosen game before advancing. Optional notes can hold a score or explanation. Completed winners cannot be silently overwritten; cancel an active tournament and create a new draw when a correction requires rebuilding it. Canceled draws remain visible and cannot advance. Draws support 2–64 eligible players who opted in before closure, including the optional instructor competitor. Multiple tournaments retain separate entrants, brackets and champions on the same date. A new additive migration preserves original tournaments on their prior single-elimination engine and moves foreign keys to the private competitor registry. The registry is service-only and never returns instructor email/subject to peers. Different-subject instructor sessions cannot manage their own competitor’s match to bypass confirmation.

Double-elimination references: [Challonge competition formats](https://kb.challonge.com/en/article/learn-about-challonge-competition-formats-1f8j1cf/) and [SNK World Championship 2026 official rulebook](https://www.snk-corp.co.jp/swc2026/rulebook_ver1_eng.pdf). Strict double elimination plays 2N−2 matches, or 2N−1 with the championship rematch. The class app randomizes the initial draw once, then follows fixed upper/lower feeds.

## Instructor dispute resolution

A player disputing an unconfirmed challenge or tournament result creates one pending dispute, retaining the originally reported winner and notes. Both players see “Result disputed. Please find your instructor together so they can decide the winner.” Result reporting/confirmation and student challenge cancellation pause until resolution; other ready matches may continue. A dispute itself adds no win/loss and does not feed a bracket.

The verified class owner gets a Resolve disputes shortcut and a queue of pending disputes, including records beyond ordinary recent-history limits. Each entry shows both players, the game/tournament and original report. The instructor selects one of the actual players, optionally writes decision notes and resolves. Challenges complete; tournament decisions use the existing single/double-elimination advancement, including championship reset. Exact retries are idempotent, and conflicting requests cannot overwrite a decision. Instructor cancellation closes outstanding disputes without a winner. If the instructor opted in as a competitor, they may explicitly decide a disputed own matchup; ordinary self-reported results still require opponent confirmation, and a changed provider subject cannot bypass that identity binding.

Players retain a Resolved by instructor notice, the decision/notes and an expandable original Dispute record. A personal decision summary stays above the next tournament opponent or elimination message, so advancing rounds does not hide the ruling. Raw reporter/disputant/decision-maker email and provider subject stay in a separate RLS, service-only audit table; row responses expose only whitelisted summaries. Cross-class/unauthorized decisions, stale reports/confirmations, repeated disputes, cancellation races and archived writes are rejected. The browser upgrades atomically in place without reseeding saved history. Production migrations remain unapplied.
